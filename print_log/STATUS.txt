THE NOZZLE SPLIT -- 0.4 AND 0.6 ARE NOT INTERCHANGEABLE
--------------------------------------------------------
Everything in bow_prusa/gcode/ is HF 0.4 / 0.25 mm except three files:
#001, #004 and #011, which carry the _0.6n_0.32mm_ASA suffix -- plus #015,
sliced 2026-09-01.

A mismatched file DOES NOT PRINT WRONG, IT REFUSES TO START. The profile's
start G-code emits

    M862.1 P0.6 A0 F0 ; nozzle check

and the Core One halts on that line before the first layer. So a 0.4 file on
the 0.6 machine is a wasted trip, not a ruined part.

PLACEMENT.txt says "28 chunks ready for a USB stick". That is true only for
the HF 0.4 machine. For the 0.6, re-slice from
gcode/PROFILE_0.6n_0.32mm_ASA.ini -- it carries support_material=0,
binary_gcode=1, 18 mm outer brim, 1 perimeter, 8% gyroid, ASA 275 / bed 110.
Budget 16% more ASA and about 27% less time than the 0.4 figures.

CRACKS ON #032  (found 2026-09-01)
----------------------------------
#032 printed to completion but came off the plate with cracks. Extent and
location NOT yet recorded -- write them down here.

It had the best first-layer contact in the bow (162 cm2) and did NOT lift, so
this is not an adhesion failure. Cracks in ASA on a part that stuck are almost
always layer splitting from thermal stress: chamber too cool, or a draft. #032
is 240 mm on its long side and 205 mm tall, which is where that shows up first.

  BEFORE REPRINTING, decide whether it matters. This chunk gets bonded on every
  cut face and the hull is glassed inside and out, so a crack that does not
  cross a dowel bore or a mating face may be structurally irrelevant once it is
  in the boat. A crack THROUGH a bore or a bond face is not.

  Check: the four cut faces, the six dowel bores, and whether any crack runs
  full depth. Photograph it either way.

  If it needs reprinting it goes back on the X1C -- #032 is one of the eight
  bow chunks that fit no way up on the Core One. Raise the chamber temperature
  and keep the door shut before rerunning it.

  Same exposure applies to its unprinted twin #029 and the other tall 240 mm
  chunks: #030, #031, #038, #039, #041, #042.

THE #007 PROBLEM
----------------
Two failures, two different modes:

  1st  supports collapsed - nine needle towers under 1.5 cm2 of overhang
       split into nine slivers. Fixed by turning supports off.
  2nd  ran nearly to the end of ~4 h and let go.

A part that survives four hours and dies at the top is not an overhang
problem. It is the part coming off the plate. #007 stands 223 mm tall on
17 cm2 of first-layer contact - ASA shrinks the whole way up, and the peeling
force at the base grows with height until 17 cm2 is not enough.

Brim cannot fix it. #007 is 240 mm on a 256 mm bed, so 8 mm is the widest
brim that physically fits, and it already has that.

USE A RAFT FOR ATTEMPT 3. A raft expands only about 2 mm sideways, so it fits
where a brim cannot, and it lays down the FULL 182 cm2 footprint instead of the
17 cm2 the curved hull face happens to touch. Ten times the grip, in the same
bed space. In Bambu Studio that is the raft layer count under Support; leave
support type off but set raft layers to 4 or so.

Same reasoning applies to any 240 mm chunk that starts letting go:
#006, #014, #015, #029, #030, #031, #032, #034, #035, #038, #039, #041, #042.
All of them are brim-capped at 8 mm.

HOW TO IDENTIFY WHAT IS ON THE SHELF
------------------------------------
Every chunk in the bottom layer has a MIRROR TWIN of identical size and weight.
Size tells you which PAIR you are holding; it cannot tell you which of the two.
For that, look at which way the curve runs -- or just label them as they come
off the plate from here on, which is cheaper than working it out later.

pair          size mm                   g   where it sits in the boat
#001 / #004   133 x 181 x 205         122   aft end, outboard, at the x=60 joint
#002 / #003   223 x 193 x 240         227   aft end, inboard - the two biggest
#005 / #008    81 x  90 x 204          23   outboard, second station forward
#006 / #007   148 x 240 x 223          95   inboard, second station forward
#009 / #010    91 x 232 x 223          65   inboard, third station forward
#011 / #012    86 x  26 x  74           5   small slivers, unmistakable

#011/#012 are the giveaway: nothing else in the bow is a 26 mm sliver. If you
cannot find a 5 g part, they are not printed. Likewise #005/#008 are the only
81 x 90 pair.

Weights are from the model, which runs about 9% under what the slicer reports,
so a scale will read a little high. Use them to tell 23 g from 227 g, not to
tell #005 from #008.
