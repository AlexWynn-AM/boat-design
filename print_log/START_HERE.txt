ON THE CORE ONE  --  bow_prusa/
--------------------------------------------------------------------
30 bow chunks that fit the Prusa's smaller plate, already placed for it.
~2.2 kg, about 35 h at the Prusa profile's 16 mm3/s cap. boatASA-8
throughout. Presets: print_profiles/prusa/.

These are the SAME X1C chunks -- same cut, same dowel pattern -- so they bond
to everything else here exactly as designed. 19 are byte-identical copies out
of bow/; 11 were turned about Z only, which does not tilt anything, so they
still stand on the same face with their dowel bores on the same axes.

  Read bow_prusa/PLACEMENT.txt at the machine. It has the settings, the four
  hard chunks, and the seams that have to stay dry until the X1C catches up.

Eight bow chunks do NOT fit the Core One and stay on the X1C:
  #013, #016, #029, #032, #038-#039, #041-#042

DO NOT print the same chunk on both machines, and do not run slice_for_print
with --printer coreone for this boat: that re-cuts the hull on a different
grid and mates with nothing already printed.

BALANCE. The Prusa profile is capped at 16 mm3/s against the Bambu's 20, so
giving it 30 of the 38 remaining bow chunks makes the slower machine the
bottleneck -- 35 h against 15. Moving #043, #044, #030, #031 and #036 back to
the X1C lands both at about 24 h. Fitting a 0.6 HF nozzle on the Core One
roughly doubles its cap and settles the question outright.


SUPPORTS  --  OFF for almost everything, and this is why
--------------------------------------------------------------------
#007 failed on the X1C on 2026-08-31 with its supports collapsing. That was
not the printer, the filament or the temperature. It was the supports being
asked to do something impossible.

#007 has 1.5 cm2 of overhang in total, and it is not one patch -- it is NINE
separate slivers, the biggest 0.32 cm2, spread over 218 mm of height. A slicer
asked to support that builds nine needles about 5 mm across and up to 218 mm
tall. At 40:1 they fall over, and when one falls it takes the print with it.

This is not special to #007. Measured over all 44 bow chunks:

  37 chunks   overhang is ONLY sub-2 cm2 slivers, 5 to 12 of them each,
              towers up to 234 mm tall            ->  SUPPORTS OFF
   7 chunks   overhang is a real consolidated patch ->  supports ON
              #013, #016, #027-#029, #043-#044

So: turn supports OFF by default. Switch them on only for those seven.

What you give up by turning them off is 1 to 2 cm2 of slightly saggy underside
per chunk, in slivers a few millimetres across, on a part whose entire job is
to be abraded and glassed. What you get back is a print that finishes.

  Reprint #007 with supports off. Nothing else about it needs changing.

The four the Core One is printing with supports (#027, #028, #043, #044) were
checked against the same measure and they are the good case: their overhang is
ONE block each -- 23 cm2 of the 23.8 on #027, 51.7 of the 52.4 on #043 -- so
the support is a broad slab with a real footprint, not a needle. Those G-code
files are correct as sliced; leave them alone.

WHY THE OVERHANG IS FRAGMENTED. orient_chunks.py picks the stance that
minimises TOTAL overhang area. It has no notion of whether what is left is one
supportable block or a scatter of slivers, and on a hull surface the minimum
usually comes out as a scatter. Minimising the number worked; it just did not
mean what it looked like it meant.


DOWELS
--------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2 mm holes for 4.0 mm printed dowels, 6 mm into each side. Print
000_dowel.stl on BOTH machines and dry-fit both ways before gluing anything:
a tight fit on one printer is only an assumption on the other.

If they bind, do NOT open the holes -- that obsoletes every chunk on both
machines. Raise DOWEL_SHRINK in slice_for_print.py and reprint the dowel
alone. It is the cheap half of the joint.

Abrade and solvent-wipe every mating face. ASA to epoxy is the least forgiving
bond on the boat.
